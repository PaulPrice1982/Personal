## WHAT A BUYER WILL ACTUALLY TEST

Commercial and financial diligence teams treat your ARR as a starting hypothesis. Every claim in the equity story gets one of five tests.

**Reality.** Is the ARR real, contracted and recurring? They rebuild it from invoices, customer by customer.

**Durability.** Will it renew? They split NRR into price and volume and read it by cohort and by acquisition.

**Repeatability.** Can new ARR be bought predictably? They look at how quota attainment is spread across the team, not the average.

**Predictability.** Does management know next quarter? They put eight quarters of forecast next to eight quarters of actuals.

**Upside.** What's left in the contracts? Uplifts not applied, usage not billed. If you don't claim it, the buyer does, in their model, at their price.

## FIVE THINGS A BUYER PAYS FOR

(Five cards.)

**Revenue integrity.** One ARR number, rebuilt from billing, closed every month end and reconciled to contract and cash.

**Retention and expansion.** Every material renewal owned 180 days out, health scored from usage, NRR reported with and without price.

**Growth engine.** A capacity model that ties to the plan, attainment shown as a distribution rather than an average, and revenue per head moving onto the trajectory a buyer expects for a business your size.

**Forecast and deal discipline.** Stage criteria based on what the buyer has done, not what the seller has. A weekly forecast call with written rules. A deal desk for every material deal.

**Commercial governance and monetisation.** Every contract abstracted. Uplifts applied where the contract allows them, usage matched to entitlement, customers moved onto current terms at renewal. Your account managers have the conversations. We show them how.

## QUESTIONS

(Accordion, same component as other service pages.)

**How is this different from vendor due diligence?**
Vendor diligence describes the business to a buyer. This changes the business before the report is written, so there's less for it to qualify. We work alongside your VDD and quality of earnings providers, not instead of them.

**Who has the customer conversations?**
Your people. We build the evidence, the uplift matrix and the scripts, and coach the team. Nothing goes to a customer on our letterhead.

**Won't uplifts push customers out?**
They can. That's why gross retention is a guardrail on every commercial lever we touch. A buyer will look for a churn spike after any price programme. We'd rather they didn't find one.

**Do you also do buy-side diligence?**
Yes, but never on both sides of the same deal. If we've prepared a business for sale, we won't diligence it for a buyer.

**How is it priced?**
A fixed fee for the assessment, a monthly retainer for the install, and a fixed fee for the final six months. On the contract book, part of the fee can be tied to recovered recurring revenue, paid only if retention holds.

**Who does the work?**
Paul Price, from the first call to the last data room question. We take on a small number of install programmes at a time.
