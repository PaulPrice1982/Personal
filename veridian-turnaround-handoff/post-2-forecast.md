# The forecast that moves every week

*Paul Price, Veridian Revenue Partners*

Most businesses can tell you last quarter's number. Fewer can tell you, with confidence, what will happen next quarter and why.

You can see the problem in one symptom: the forecast moves every week. Week one it is 95 of plan. Week three it is 88. Week five it is back to 94 because a big deal "came back". Nobody panics, because everyone has learned that the number wobbles. That learned tolerance is the real damage, because when the forecast finally falls off a cliff, it looks like every other wobble until it is too late to act.

## Nobody measures the miss

Here is the question that exposes it: how far off was the forecast last time, by seller and by manager?

Almost nobody can answer. The quarter closes, the number is whatever it is, and the next quarter starts. Nobody goes back and asks who called their business right, who sandbagged, who was optimistic by a third. So nobody learns, and the forecast moves again.

Forecast accuracy and bias are measurable. A seller who lands within ten per cent of their call, quarter after quarter, earns the right to be believed. A manager whose roll-up is always fifteen per cent high is not lying, they are hoping, and once you can see the bias you can correct for it mechanically. This is not a CRM field. It is a discipline.

## Triangulate, then trust

A forecast you can act on is built from three independent views:

- What the pipeline maths says: coverage, stage conversion, cycle times, measured on your own history rather than industry folklore.
- What the sellers commit: their names against their numbers.
- What the pattern says: this quarter's shape compared with the last eight.

When the three views agree, you can invest ahead of the number. When they disagree, the disagreement tells you exactly where to look, deal by deal. Either way the conversation changes from "what is the number" to "why is the number".

## The cadence is the fix

None of this is a project. It is a cadence: a weekly look at pipeline health, a weekly look at what will actually close, a monthly look at whether the fixes are working. An hour a week, run with discipline, beats a quarterly deep-dive every time, because the forecast is a muscle and muscles are built by repetition.

Stop guessing the quarter. Start running it.
