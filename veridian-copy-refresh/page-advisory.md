## THE EXPENSIVE MISTAKE

Depending on whose research you read, a CRO lasts somewhere between 17 and 25 months. Harvard Business Review found that 62% of companies saw growth flatten or fall in the year after one left. Add the search fee, the package, the equity and the two best reps who follow them out of the door.

It's rarely the person. Nobody had defined the ICP, the stages, the forecast method or the comp plan, so the new CRO spends year one building them. Meanwhile they're judged on a number they had no chance of hitting.

Then there's the contract book, which almost no board looks at. Customers still on terms from 2019. Renewals at last year's price. Usage well past what's licensed, and well past what's invoiced. That gap rarely makes the board pack, and when it does nobody owns it. Every acquisition brings its own paper and widens it.

Close both and the first few points of growth come back from customers you already have.

## WATCH WHAT MOVES FIRST

Bookings and ARR are history by the time they move. These shift about six months earlier, so they're what we put in front of the board:

- Pipeline coverage that only counts opportunities younger than one sales cycle with a dated next step. Most CRMs flatter this badly.
- Forecast accuracy measured on day one of the quarter. Under 85% and the plan is a hope.
- Win rate from qualified opportunity, split by segment.
- Days spent in each stage. Take a day out of every stage and it compounds across a year of deals.
- Product usage across the base, six months before each renewal.
- How many large deals closed without the CEO on the call.
